What Gruha can and can’t do

Last updated 14 November 2026

Gruha shows a furniture or décor product inside a photo of the shopper’s own room. It is an AI render of that photo, not augmented reality and not a photograph, and the result depends on the photo it is given.

In short

  • It’s a visualisation, not a photograph.
  • A daylit indoor photo gives a much better result than a dim or outdoor one.
  • When a room has nowhere sensible for a large piece, it says so and asks for a better photo rather than inventing a spot.
  • The room comes back unchanged, and prices come from your real catalogue.

What it does

  • Places your product in the shopper’s own room photo at its real size, lit by the light already in that room.
  • Leaves the rest of the room unchanged.
  • Places products automatically, with no dragging or resizing for the shopper.
  • Handles several kinds of placement: on a surface (a bowl on a coffee table), against a wall (a floor mirror), in place of something already there (a side table swap), and several pieces in one room at once.
  • Returns an itemised quote in ₹ with real prices from your catalogue and a buy link to your product page.

What it doesn’t do

  • It is not a photograph and not a live camera view. It is one rendered image, made from your catalogue photograph and the shopper’s room photograph.
  • It does not invent a spot. When a room has nowhere sensible for a large piece, it says so and asks for a better photo.
  • It does not need 3D models, so it also does not offer a 3D view you can turn around.
  • Results vary with the room. We test on your products, in real rooms, before anything goes live.

What makes a good room photo

  • An indoor room in daylight. A daylit indoor photo gives a much better result than a dim or outdoor one.
  • The room the shopper is actually buying for, bare or already lived-in.
  • Some free floor or wall where the piece could really stand. If there is none for a large piece, it will ask for another photo.

Shoppers see a short photo guide before they upload, and can use the phone camera directly or pick a photo from the gallery.

How real size is kept

Each product is set up with its real dimensions, and the render places it at that size in the shopper’s room. On the homepage, an entryway console with a stated width of 106 cm comes out at about 108 cm, narrower than the console it replaced. Small pieces come out at their real size.

Photos and privacy

Consent is taken before any photo is sent, and the consent box is never pre-ticked. Shoppers’ photos are deleted after 90 days. The privacy note has the details.

Questions

Is it accurate?

It is a visualisation, not a photograph. The product is placed at its real size, and small pieces come out at their real size, but results vary with the room photo. We test on your products before anything goes live.

What happens if the room has no space for the piece?

When a room has nowhere sensible for a large piece, it says so and asks for a better photo rather than inventing a spot.

Is a failed render charged?

No. A failed render is free.
