The problem

Customers can’t picture it in their own space.

A white-background product shot tells them what it looks like. It does not tell them whether it fits, whether it suits the room, or what it costs once it is in there.

  • “Will it fit?” Dimensions in centimetres are not a picture of your hallway.
  • “Will it suit my room?” A studio shot shares no light, no floor, no walls with their home.
  • “I’ll think about it.” The browser tab closes and the cart stays empty.
  • Every one of those is answered by one photograph of the room they already own.

Honest limits

What it does, and what it doesn’t.

It’s a visualisation, not a photograph. A daylit indoor shot gives a much better result than a dim or outdoor one — and when a room has nowhere sensible for a large piece, it says so and asks for a better photo rather than inventing a spot.

  • Your room comes back unchanged
  • Real prices from your real catalogue
  • Small pieces come out at their real size
