The problem

Customers can’t picture it in their own space.

A white-background product shot tells them what it looks like. It does not tell them whether it fits, whether it suits the room, or what it costs once it is in there.

  • “Will it fit?” Dimensions in centimetres are not a picture of your hallway.
  • “Will it suit my room?” A studio shot shares no light, no floor, no walls with their home.
  • “I’ll think about it.” The browser tab closes and the cart stays empty.
  • Every one of those is answered by one photograph of the room they already own.

FAQ

Questions brands ask first.

What the button is, what it needs from you, and what it costs.

What is a “See it in your room” button?

One button on your product page. A customer photographs their room, and your product appears in it at its real size, lit by their own light, with your price and a link straight to your checkout.

Do I need 3D models?

No. It runs on your existing catalogue photos: each render is generated from your catalogue photograph and the shopper’s room photograph.

Does it work on Shopify and other websites?

Yes. On Shopify it is an app you switch on from your theme editor. On WooCommerce or any other site it is one script tag.

How long does a render take?

About a minute, from your customer’s phone photo to a priced room.

What does it cost?

Starter is ₹4,999 per month for 5,000 credits (about 125 renders). Growth is ₹9,999 per month for 10,000 credits (about 250 renders), and White-label is ₹17,999 per month for 10,000 credits with no Gruha badge. One render is 40 credits, a failed render is free, and prices exclude GST; the pricing page has the details.

Will it change my store’s design?

No. Nothing in your theme is edited, and it comes off just as easily.

What can’t it do?

It’s a visualisation, not a photograph. A daylit indoor photo gives a much better result than a dim or outdoor one, and when a room has nowhere sensible for a large piece, it says so and asks for a better photo rather than inventing a spot. More on what it can and can’t do.
